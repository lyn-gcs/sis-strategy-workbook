01 / How to use Totalastic

HOW TOTALASTIC™ WORKS

Use one response sequence without replacing the existing SIS learning ecosystem.

TOTALASTIC™ IMPLEMENTATION WORKBOOK

How SEG and SIS apply Totalastic™ and Let Learning Shine in everyday practice.

Total + Elastic. Develop the whole person through 360º total character development, strong academics, wellbeing and human connection. Adapt the route, support and opportunities without abandoning standards, identity or values. [S1, pp.10, 53-54; S2, pp.12, 15]

Look through Ask What this changes
The Person Who is this person becoming? Consider learning, character, wellbeing, critical thinking and purpose.
The People Who do they grow with? Use relationships, belonging, culture and parent-school partnership.
The Passport Where could this growth lead? Connect skills, credentials, networks and future progression.

Use the same six steps

01 NOTICE: Name the change, strength, opportunity or concern. Use an observation, not a label.

02 UNDERSTAND: Ask the learner and check relevant evidence. Do not infer a cause from one mark or behaviour.

03 RESPOND: Agree one useful action. Keep the intended learning standard clear.

04 CONNECT: Involve only people needed to deliver the action. Name one lead.

05 FOLLOW THROUGH: Set a review date. Continue, change, close or escalate.

06 MAKE GROWTH VISIBLE: Identify one useful sign of progress. Evidence can be a conversation, demonstration or existing piece of work.

Learners × Educators: A student learns a concept; the teacher learns which explanation helps. Both use the next attempt to improve. This is the existing relationship, not another branded programme. [S2, pp.9-12]

Example: A student avoids fractions. Notice two unfinished tasks; ask where they get stuck; model one example with fraction strips; agree three independent questions; connect with the homeroom teacher only if the concern is broader; revisit next week; compare independent reasoning, not just completion.

Let Learning Shine: Make learning, development and potential visible to the right person. A private explanation counts; a public performance is not compulsory.

Use and status: These are proposed operating practices for approval. Scenario names and situations are illustrative, not SIS case records. Match functional roles to current staff before use. Safeguarding, assessment and employment policies take priority. Proposed review intervals are starting points, not legal deadlines.

Use by / timing / tool: All staff, during existing work; choose a relevant page, then T2 only when follow-up needs a record.

Suggested visual: Three existing lenses above a six-step horizontal response strip; retain the source architecture.

02 / How to use Totalastic

Decide who acts and when to escalate

Match the response to the situation, including when ordinary follow-up must stop.

Start with safety, not the review calendar

Possible harm, abuse, serious distress or immediate danger? Follow the school's safeguarding or emergency procedure immediately. Contact the designated safeguarding function; use the emergency route when needed. Do not wait for a pattern or the next meeting. Staff do not investigate disclosures, promise secrecy or diagnose a condition. Keep safeguarding records out of growth portfolios. Parent contact is determined through the safeguarding procedure where contact could increase risk. [R14]

Ordinary learning situation Lead and next action Record and review
One small, isolated difficulty Subject/CCA teacher: clarify, support, check next attempt. Existing work may be enough; no new case form.
Repeated or across settings Homeroom teacher coordinates with relevant teachers. One short action note; review in 1-2 school weeks.
Broader or persistent support need Pastoral/academic lead arranges the appropriate qualified support. Existing support plan; agree parent involvement and date.
Significant unresolved concern Relevant school leader reviews resources, responsibilities and next route. Record decision, owner and next update; do not leave open-ended.

Three quick decisions

ROUTE-01: One poor quiz. Check the questions and talk to the student; reteach the missed concept; check the next task. Teacher leads. Do not automatically book a parent meeting. Close when the concept is demonstrated independently; widen the review if the difficulty repeats.

ROUTE-02: Withdrawal in several lessons. Teachers share factual observations with homeroom; homeroom checks privately with the student and consults pastoral support. Agree one support action and an early review. A safety concern immediately leaves the ordinary route above.

ROUTE-03: Strong new talent. Ask whether the learner wants to explore it; offer a small stretch task; connect to a suitable teacher or activity. Review after the trial. Stop the extra activity if the learner declines or it adds unhelpful pressure; no deficit referral is needed.

Handover rule: The original lead keeps responsibility until the receiving colleague confirms ownership and the next action. A forwarded message is not a completed handover.

Closure rule: Close the additional action when the agreed outcome is demonstrated and ordinary support is sufficient. Note the outcome and reopening trigger; keep records under existing retention rules, not indefinitely by default.

Connection: The Person receives proportionate care; The People share responsibility; The Passport remains open without premature labels.

Tool / frequency: T2, only for coordinated follow-up; decisions in the moment.

Suggested visual: Safety override above a four-branch decision tree; closure and handover at the bottom.

03 / How to use Totalastic

Give every action an owner and a rhythm

Put the workbook inside current roles, records and routines.

Assign functions, not new job titles

Function Responsibility
Subject / CCA teacher Notice, support, check the next attempt and share relevant patterns.
Homeroom / mentor Keep the whole-learner view and coordinate cross-subject follow-up.
Academic lead Protect standards, support teaching and oversee assessment questions.
Pastoral / safeguarding Arrange specialist support and manage protected processes.
Pathway adviser Check options, requirements, deadlines and learner choices.
School leadership Approve responsibilities, workload, policies and unresolved decisions.
AO / operations Route enquiries, manage logistics and confirm handovers; do not interpret grades or diagnose.
SEG functional leads Provide shared capability, quality controls, resources and partner delivery.

Before use: Principal names the actual staff covering these functions, their deputies, contact routes and decision limits. One person may hold several functions. No new headcount is assumed.

Use existing time

In the moment: Ask a clarifying question, adapt an explanation, recognise a contribution or use the safety route.

Daily: Greet and notice; complete agreed handovers; give students ordinary chances to ask for help.

Weekly / fortnightly: Use current homeroom time for reflection; review active actions in existing pastoral or departmental time. Do not discuss every student every week.

Termly: Review learner goals through current reports; complete one manageable teacher growth cycle; assess participation and partner usefulness.

Yearly: Review policy, staffing, budget, pathway information, staff development and partner agreements.

Keep one useful record

The growth snapshot is an index, not a second student database. Link to existing work and plans. The action note contains only observation, action, owner, review date and outcome. Update it when something changes; do not log every interaction. Restrict sensitive information to the authorised system.

Workload test: Before adding a task, name what it replaces, the time needed and who has that time. Test with two staff members; remove duplicate entry before wider use.

Good implementation: Staff and learners know the next step; reviews happen; classroom work remains the main work.

Connection: The People makes care dependable; The Person receives useful support; The Passport benefits from reliable transitions. Educators are learners too.

Tools: T1-T10 are optional field sets for existing documents, not ten compulsory forms.

Suggested visual: Owner matrix beside a five-level frequency ladder; one-record rule in a callout.

05 / SIS - School system

Apply Totalastic™ to school decisions

Make policies and resource choices support care and challenge together.

Use the existing approval process

1. Name the learning or service outcome and the people affected.

2. Check academic standards, safety, fairness, access and workload.

3. Ask whether the route can change while the outcome stays clear.

4. Name the decision owner, explain the decision and set a review point.

Apply the existing SEG values: Integrity means give accurate reasons; Excellence means retain the standard; Fairness means check who cannot access the opportunity; Empathy means understand the effect on the person. These are decision prompts, not a new scoring system. [S1, p.66]

SYS-01 | A homework rule creates an access barrier

Notice / understand: A student repeatedly misses online homework. Speak privately; establish whether the issue is understanding, access, competing demands or another cause. Do not assume laziness or hardship.

Respond: 1. Teacher provides an equivalent school-based or paper route while checking the barrier. 2. Agree what the student will complete and by when. 3. Academic lead checks whether the same barrier affects others and whether guidance needs clarification.

People / parents: Teacher leads the first response; AO can arrange access. Involve parents where they can help establish a workable arrangement, without disclosing another family's circumstances.

Review / close / escalate: Check the next two tasks. Close extra follow-up when the route works. Refer repeated non-completion to the academic/homeroom lead; safety concerns use p.2.

Evidence / good: The student completes the same learning objective through an accessible route. Submission expectations remain explicit.

Connection: The Person: learning habits. The People: fair, respectful support. The Passport: access to continued study. Shine: Let the student explain the completed learning; no public account of the barrier.

Apply the same check elsewhere

Policy: Keep required outcomes and approved exceptions clear.

Recognition: Include improvement and contribution without changing academic awards' criteria.

Communication: Use one agreed message and named contact; AO routes questions to the owner.

Calendar: Check combined assessment, trip and event demands before confirming dates.

Resources: Protect essential learning/support before optional display or celebration costs.

Lead / timing: Principal owns policy; delegated leads act within agreed limits. Review at existing termly planning and when a material barrier appears.

Tool: T10 decision fields; T2 only for the individual action needing review.

Suggested visual: Four-question approval gate with SYS-01 beside it; no extra committee.

Basis / reference: S1, pp.10, 65-66; S2, p.15. R14 (p.65) supports the separate safeguarding route.

06 / SIS - School system

Handle behaviour and competing school demands

Use boundaries and shared planning without treating every matter as a referral.

SYS-02 | A student repeatedly interrupts others

Notice / understand: Note the behaviour, time and effect. Ask the student privately what was happening; distinguish a single interruption from a pattern. Check whether instructions or participation arrangements are contributing.

Respond: 1. Stop the interruption calmly and restate the expected behaviour. 2. Apply the existing behaviour policy proportionately; agree one replacement action, such as recording a question until a pause. 3. Repair any impact appropriately and check the next lesson. Do not compel a harmed student to meet or accept an apology.

People / parents: Teacher leads; homeroom joins a repeated cross-lesson pattern. Inform parents when a continuing plan needs home support or policy requires it, not for every reminder.

Review / close / escalate: Review after one school week. Close additional monitoring when respectful participation is sustained. Pastoral/academic lead reviews persistent difficulty; bullying, discrimination or safety concerns follow the relevant policy immediately.

Evidence / good: Others can contribute; the student participates without repeated disruption. Record behaviour and response, not a permanent character label.

Connection / Shine: The Person learns self-management; The People protects belonging. Recognise specific improvement privately, not a public behaviour ranking.

SYS-03 | A performance clashes with assessment and support time

Notice / understand: The event requires rehearsals during revision and agreed support sessions. Check dates, affected students, transport, staff cover and the actual benefit.

Respond: 1. Event lead maps the clash with academic and pastoral leads. 2. Move or shorten rehearsals, offer another contribution route and protect essential support. 3. Principal resolves any remaining trade-off; AO issues one clear revised schedule.

People / parents: Event lead owns delivery; academic lead protects assessment preparation. Inform affected families of changed commitments; ask for input only where transport or participation depends on them.

Review / close / escalate: Check the revised plan before publishing and review after the event. Close when missed learning/support is restored. Escalate unresolved staffing, access or safety problems before proceeding; cancel an element that cannot be delivered safely.

Evidence / good: Students can participate without silently losing required learning or support. Staff know the final timetable.

Connection / Shine: The Person has balanced development; The People share fair access; The Passport retains academic preparation. Show contributions beyond the most visible performers.

Timing / tools: In the moment for behaviour; during existing planning for events. T2 for repeated behaviour; existing calendar and T10 for decisions.

Suggested visual: Two parallel case cards; separate behaviour boundary from calendar decision.

Basis: S1, pp.10, 65-66; S2, p.15.

07 / SIS - Learners: students

Know the learner before choosing the support

Use the whole-person view to choose one practical next step.

Ask only what changes the action

The Person: What can the student do? What is difficult? What do they enjoy or care about?

The People: Where do they feel included? Which adult or peer relationship helps?

The Passport: Which skill or opportunity might this enable next? For younger learners, this means exploration, not choosing a career.

Use the student's voice and existing work. A learner profile should not become a personality label, diagnosis or catalogue of family details. Keep changing interests open. [S2, pp.9-12, 15]

STU-01 | A student has stopped trying in Maths

Notice / understand: Two tasks are blank; the student says, "I always get it wrong." Ask them to show where they stopped. Check a previous successful task and the current difficulty.

Respond: 1. Identify one missing step, rather than reteaching everything. 2. Model it and complete one example together. 3. Let the student attempt a similar question independently; agree one attainable goal for the next lesson.

People / parents: Maths teacher leads. Homeroom joins only if confidence is falling elsewhere. Invite parent support when useful, such as a calm agreed practice routine; do not prescribe extra tuition automatically.

Review / close / escalate: Revisit next lesson and after one week. Reduce scaffolding when the student can explain the method. Widen the review if avoidance persists or other concerns emerge.

Evidence / good: The student attempts work and can identify a strategy that helps. Correctness and confidence are both discussed; confidence is not inferred from smiling.

Connection: The Person: confidence and competence. The People: trusted teacher care. The Passport: a stronger foundation for future learning.

Let Learning Shine: Compare an earlier attempt with the current explanation privately. Ask, "What can you now do without my help?"

Lead / timing / tool: Homeroom maintains the T1 index at existing termly reviews or a significant change; subject teachers contribute only useful observations. Keep work in its original location.

Success boundary: Do not turn every preference into a special programme. A small classroom adjustment may be enough. Seek appropriate specialist advice for needs beyond the teacher's role.

Suggested visual: Three practical question columns with a worked confidence case; no new personality model.

Real-world reference: R09, Big Picture Learning Australia (p.63), shows how interests can lead to plans and evidence; retain SIS assessment standards.

08 / SIS - Learners: students

Respond to social difficulty and changed behaviour

Notice what marks alone do not show without making unsupported assumptions.

STU-02 | Strong academics, difficult group relationships

Notice / understand: A high-achieving student completes tasks but dismisses peers' suggestions. Ask the student and observe a group task; do not equate quietness or difference with poor social development.

Respond: 1. State the group expectation: explain your reasoning and respond respectfully to another idea. 2. Give rotating roles, including listening and summarising, rather than making the student permanent leader. 3. Offer specific feedback after the task and a second chance to practise.

People / parents: Class teacher leads; student helps set the goal. Homeroom joins if exclusion or conflict recurs. Parents join only for an ongoing pattern or useful support, subject to policy.

Review / close / escalate: Check two group tasks over a fortnight. Close the extra goal when collaboration is independent; involve pastoral support if relationships keep deteriorating. Address intimidation or bullying through policy, not a teamwork exercise alone.

Evidence / good: A peer's idea is accurately acknowledged and used; the student can disagree respectfully. Academic extension continues.

Connection / Shine: The Person develops empathy and judgement; The People gains reciprocal relationships; The Passport gains collaboration. Recognise a specific contribution, not a "nice person" score.

STU-03 | A usually engaged student becomes withdrawn

Notice / understand: Record factual changes in attendance, participation or behaviour. Check privately: "I noticed you left two tasks unfinished. How have things been?" Listen without suggesting a cause.

Respond: 1. Establish whether any immediate safety response is needed. 2. Agree a manageable classroom adjustment while relevant support is considered. 3. Homeroom checks whether other teachers notice a change and arranges an appropriate pastoral conversation.

People / parents: Homeroom coordinates; subject teachers share only necessary observations. Pastoral/safeguarding staff determine the support route and safe parent involvement. Do not circulate sensitive details to all staff.

Review / close / escalate: Check back within the agreed short interval, sooner if concern increases. Close additional classroom adjustments only when the learner and lead agree they are no longer needed; specialist plans have their own closure decision. Safety concerns bypass routine review.

Evidence / good: The student knows a trusted contact and can access learning safely. A return to high marks alone does not prove that the concern is resolved.

Connection / Shine: The Person: wellbeing; The People: trusted support. Growth may stay private; do not turn a vulnerable experience into a school story.

Tool / timing: T2 for coordinated action; safeguarding information stays in the protected system. Observe naturally, review only active actions.

Suggested visual: Two case cards; explicit privacy and safety flags.

Basis / reference: S2, pp.12, 15, 20; R14 (p.65).

09 / SIS - Learners: educators

Help educators learn through teaching

Treat a teacher's practice as a learning process, not only an appraisal result.

Use one small practice cycle

Choose a learner need → select one teaching move → observe or collect work → discuss what happened → adjust and retry. Keep this inside current planning and mentoring. Use the same six Totalastic steps, with the educator as the learner. [S2, pp.9-12]

EDU-01 | Strong subject knowledge, low lesson engagement

Notice / understand: The teacher explains accurately, but few students contribute and independent work is incomplete. Compare work and observe a short lesson segment; ask the teacher what they intended. Student feedback describes the experience, not a popularity rating.

Respond: 1. Agree one focus, such as checking understanding before moving on. 2. Mentor models a brief explanation followed by an all-student response and paired explanation. 3. Teacher tries it in two lessons and brings a few work samples to a short review.

People / parents: Teacher leads the trial; an agreed mentor supports; subject head protects time and standards. Parents are not involved in a confidential coaching conversation. Address any separate parent concern through the ordinary school route.

Review / close / escalate: Review after two weeks. Keep or adapt the technique based on learning, not activity levels alone. Close this cycle when the teacher uses it confidently and students show understanding; choose the next goal later. Persistent performance concerns move transparently to the existing support/appraisal process, not hidden surveillance.

Evidence / good: More students reveal their thinking and can complete the intended task; the teacher explains what changed and what still needs work.

Connection: The educator's Person: expertise and confidence. People: mentoring and learner feedback. Passport: stronger professional capability.

Let Learning Shine: Share the teaching move and anonymised before/after work with colleagues, with the teacher's agreement; not a public ranking.

System distinction: This chapter shows the teacher's own learning. Pages 21-22 explain how SIS provides onboarding, observation, appraisal and career support around that learning.

Frequency / tool: One selected focus per cycle; use existing mentoring time. T4 for evidence and T6 for the agreed goal, not two duplicate narratives.

Reference: R05 and R06 (p.61): feedback, practice and next steps make training usable.

Suggested visual: Small practice loop with a complete educator case and two sample work checkpoints.

10 / SIS - Learners: educators

Stretch strong teachers and learn from a failed trial

Make educator growth relevant to strengths as well as difficulties.

EDU-02 | A strong teacher is ready to develop others

Notice / understand: A teacher consistently helps students explain their thinking. Ask whether they want a mentoring opportunity; examine actual practice and workload, not only examination scores.

Respond: 1. Agree one bounded role: model a questioning routine for one colleague. 2. Provide a mentoring prompt and protected time; rehearse giving descriptive feedback. 3. Let the colleague try the routine and discuss what they adapted.

People / parents: Teacher and colleague co-own learning; subject head sets scope and cover. No parent involvement is needed.

Review / close / escalate: Review after four weeks. Close the trial with feedback from both teachers and learner work. Continue only with agreed time; involve the people/leadership function if a larger role, recognition or workload change is proposed.

Evidence / good: The colleague independently uses or adapts the practice; the mentor learns how to support another adult, rather than simply demonstrates expertise.

Connection / Shine: Person: adult confidence and mentoring skill; People: reciprocal peer learning; Passport: a possible specialist or leadership route. Recognise the contribution with permission, without implying promotion is guaranteed.

EDU-03 | A new teaching approach does not help

Notice / understand: A teacher tries open group discussion; confident students dominate and written answers remain weak. Check the task, instructions and work rather than concluding that the teacher or approach has failed permanently.

Respond: 1. Identify the missing condition: all students need preparation and a way to contribute. 2. Retry with individual thinking, paired explanation and a written exit response. 3. Compare understanding against the same learning objective; retain useful elements and stop ineffective ones.

People / parents: Teacher leads with optional peer feedback. Subject head joins if the trial affects required content or assessment. No routine parent communication is required.

Review / close / escalate: Review after the next two lessons. End the trial if it repeatedly reduces learning or consumes disproportionate time; agree another approach. Escalate a broader resourcing or training need, not the fact of an honest small experiment.

Evidence / good: The teacher can explain why the adjustment helped or did not help. Learner work, not enthusiasm alone, informs the decision.

Connection / Shine: Person: reflection and adaptability; People: safe professional feedback; Passport: developing professional judgement. Share an anonymised learning note rather than presenting every trial as a success.

Tool / timing: T6, one focus and one evidence link; four-week mentoring trial or short lesson trial as specified.

Suggested visual: Two equal case cards; avoid a deficit-versus-star-teacher hierarchy.

Basis / reference: S2, pp.9-12, 15; R05-R06 (p.61).

11 / SIS - Lessons

Apply Totalastic™ inside an ordinary lesson

Adapt a lesson's route while keeping its learning objective clear.

Example: an existing 40-minute lesson

Time Teacher and learner action
0-5 minutes Introduce the question and check what students already understand.
5-12 minutes Explain or model the key idea; name what a successful answer must show.
12-27 minutes Students practise. Offer a scaffold where needed and a deeper challenge where ready.
27-35 minutes Students explain, compare or apply the idea; teacher checks individual understanding.
35-40 minutes One short response: what changed in your understanding, and what is next?

The timing is illustrative, not a required lesson formula. Do not add a separate Totalastic lesson plan. Use the objective, task and feedback already being prepared.

LES-01 | Introducing a topic to a mixed-readiness class

Notice / understand: A starter reveals that some students can apply a concept while others lack its prerequisite. Check answers from everyone, not just volunteers.

Respond: 1. State the common objective and explain one model. 2. Give a short supported task to students needing the prerequisite; ask ready students to justify, compare or transfer the same concept. 3. Recheck independently and regroup flexibly for the next task.

People / parents: Subject teacher leads; a support colleague joins only if already assigned and useful. No routine parent message or ability label is needed.

Review / close / escalate: Review in the lesson and through the next piece of work. Remove the scaffold as independence develops. Seek subject/support advice for persistent barriers; do not leave a student indefinitely in the same group.

Evidence / good: Students make progress towards the objective through different levels of support and challenge. Advanced students get depth, not only extra questions.

Connection / Shine: The Person develops competence and critical thinking; The People provides responsive teacher care; The Passport builds usable foundations. Students can show how their reasoning changed.

Teacher prompts: "What makes you think that?" "What evidence would change your answer?" "Where could this idea be useful outside this task?" Let students question the example, not merely repeat it.

Tool / timing: T4 prompts inserted into existing planning; in the moment. No requirement to use every prompt each lesson.

Suggested visual: Annotated lesson strip with scaffold / deepen branches returning to one objective.

Basis / references: S2, pp.15-20; R03-R04 (p.60).

12 / SIS - Lessons

Make group work and feedback lead to learning

Give students useful ways to contribute, question and improve.

LES-02 | One student does all the group work

Notice / understand: The finished product looks strong, but only one student can explain it. Ask each student one question; check task roles and whether others had a genuine contribution.

Respond: 1. Break the task into shared thinking and individual accountability. 2. Rotate roles; each student prepares an explanation or evidence before the group decides. 3. Ask students to compare two possible answers and explain the chosen one; check each person's understanding at the end.

People / parents: Teacher leads; students agree roles. Do not make the strongest student the unpaid permanent tutor. Parents do not need an update for a routine task adjustment.

Review / close / escalate: Check the next group task. Stop formal role scaffolds when the group can work fairly without them. Refer repeated exclusion to homeroom; bullying or intimidation follows policy immediately.

Evidence / good: Every student can explain a part and the overall decision. A polished group product is not mistaken for everyone's learning.

Connection / Shine: The Person: reasoning; The People: collaboration and belonging; The Passport: shared problem-solving. Use short individual explanations, not a teamwork score for every task.

LES-03 | A student receives feedback but repeats the error

Notice / understand: Written comments have not changed the next attempt. Ask the student to explain the feedback and show what they would change; check whether the comment was understandable and actionable.

Respond: 1. Select one priority correction, such as supporting a claim with evidence. 2. Model a short improvement and allow revision during lesson time. 3. Ask the student to apply the same idea to a new example and explain the difference.

People / parents: Teacher and student lead. A peer may use a simple prompt, but the teacher checks accuracy. No new home task or parent message is automatic.

Review / close / escalate: Review the revision now and the next independent task. Close the feedback cycle when the student transfers the learning. Seek subject/support advice if understanding remains unclear after adjusted teaching.

Evidence / good: The student uses the advice in new work, not only corrects the original sentence. Their explanation shows the learning behind the edit.

Connection / Shine: The Person: reflection and critical thinking; The People: useful feedback; The Passport: transferable skills. Keep an existing before/after excerpt only when useful for reporting.

Tool / timing: T4; ordinary lessons. Existing work is the evidence; no separate observation form per child.

Suggested visual: Two case cards with small examples of individual accountability and an improved answer.

Basis / references: S2, p.15; R03-R04 (p.60).

13 / SIS - Examinations and assessment

When a Maths result falls from 75% to 52%

Use a changed result to choose support, not to jump to a cause.

The assessment rule

Keep the actual mark, learning standard and exam rules. Compare like with like: content, difficulty, marking and conditions may differ. A result is one input alongside work, attendance, the student's account and relevant wellbeing observations. Totalastic does not award extra academic marks for effort. [S2, p.15]

EXM-01 | An unexpected decline

Scenario: A Year 7 student usually scores around 75% but scores 52% in Maths.

Notice / understand: Check marking and the questions missed. Compare recent work and ask the student to explain two answers. Establish whether this is an isolated topic gap, an examination issue or part of a wider change; do not assume motivation or anxiety.

Respond: 1. Teacher selects one priority skill from the evidence. 2. Agree an achievable goal, such as independently solving a particular type of fraction problem. 3. Provide a worked model, short guided practice and an independent attempt. 4. Set a two-school-week review using similar questions that test the same skill, not the identical memorised paper.

People / parents: Maths teacher leads. Homeroom becomes involved if the change appears in other subjects or wellbeing. Contact parents where home support is useful, the concern persists, or policy requires it; one poor test does not automatically require a meeting.

Review / close / escalate: Check briefly next lesson and at the agreed review. Close the extra action when the priority skill is independently secure and normal monitoring is sufficient. If not, revise the explanation/support and involve the subject lead. Wider or safety concerns follow p.2 immediately as appropriate.

Evidence / good: The original 52% remains on record. The student can explain what changed and demonstrate the targeted skill; the teacher knows whether the intervention helped. Do not promise a particular next-test percentage.

Connection: The Person: academic growth and confidence. The People: teacher and, where needed, mentor/parent support. The Passport: protecting progression through secure foundations.

Let Learning Shine: Compare one earlier error with a later independent explanation. The improvement need not become an assembly story.

Instead of: Repeated informal help with no agreed goal or review.

Use: One identified need → one action → one owner → one review → a clear outcome.

Tool / timing: T2 if support needs follow-up; existing marked work is linked, not copied. Two weeks is a proposed ordinary review interval, never a safety waiting period.

Suggested visual: Result-to-response flow with a visible parent-decision branch.

EXM-02 | Above 90%, but unwilling to speak

Notice / understand: Consistently strong marks coexist with avoiding whole-class speaking. Ask privately whether the student wants help contributing; check comfort in pairs and other settings. Silence alone is not a disorder or lack of learning.

EXM-04 | Results remain consistently low

Notice / understand: Review the pattern by skill, not just total marks. Check prior knowledge, access to instructions, attendance, teaching opportunities and the student's explanation. Do not infer fixed ability.

Respond: 1. Identify the smallest prerequisite blocking progress. 2. Arrange short targeted instruction within existing support and keep access to the wider curriculum. 3. Recheck a matched independent task; record which support actually helps.

People / parents: Subject teacher leads with subject/support staff. Homeroom coordinates wider issues. Involve parents to understand barriers and agree useful support, not to blame or prescribe tutoring by default.

Review / close / escalate: Review after two school weeks, sooner if appropriate. Close the targeted action when the prerequisite is secure; retain any broader support still needed. Evidence / good: Specific skills improve and the next step is known, even before the overall mark rises. Lowering the intended curriculum expectation is not the default response.

PAS-02 | A student keeps setting goals but does not act
